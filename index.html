GLpatches — Over-the-counter GLP-1-inspired skin patches marketed as a weight-loss aid

GLpatches are over-the-counter adhesive skin patches sold as "GLP-1" or berberine patches and marketed as weight-loss aids. Despite the name, they do not contain actual GLP-1 medications such as semaglutide or tirzepatide — they are non-prescription topical stickers positioned as a convenient alternative for people curious about GLP-1-style weight support. Typical formulas lean on plant-derived ingredients like berberine, green tea extract and chromium that are commonly associated with metabolic and appetite-support marketing. They are applied to the skin, are not swallowed, and are not a substitute for prescribed medication or professional medical advice.

What Are The Ingredients In GLpatches?

Berberine

A yellow plant compound frequently used in blood-sugar and metabolism marketing; it is the ingredient most often tied to the "berberine patch" nickname for these products.

Green Tea Extract

A concentrated extract of green tea leaves containing catechins and small amounts of caffeine, commonly included in weight-management formulas for its metabolism-support reputation.

Chromium

A mineral often paired with weight-management products because it is marketed as supporting normal blood-sugar metabolism and helping curb sugar cravings.

Garcinia Cambogia

An extract from a tropical fruit containing hydroxycitric acid (HCA), a long-standing ingredient in appetite and weight-management supplement marketing.

L-Carnitine

A compound the body uses in fat metabolism; it appears in many slimming and energy-support formulas as a fat-transport helper.

Forskolin

An extract from the coleus plant marketed in weight-management circles for its alleged role in fat metabolism and lean-body support.

No. GLpatches are over-the-counter adhesive patches and do not contain prescription GLP-1 drugs such as semaglutide or tirzepatide. The name refers to the marketing angle, not the contents.

Peel the patch from its backing and press it onto clean, dry skin — commonly the upper arm, shoulder or abdomen — for the period stated on the packaging, then remove and replace it. Follow the label directions rather than guessing.

Because patches are non-medicated lifestyle products, there is no guaranteed timeline. Some users report noticing appetite changes within a few weeks, but results depend heavily on diet, activity and consistency.
